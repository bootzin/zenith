# Zenith Plus

Version 0.12.16

The part of Zenith that needs scripts. Zenith's six mods work without it; Plus adds what CSS alone can't do. It needs a script loader: Sine, or fx-autoconfig installed by hand.

**Peek**: quick lookups that leave nothing behind. Alt+Shift+click a link to peek it (Keys › Peek: Ctrl+Alt, Alt instead of Zen's Glance, or swapped: Alt peeks and Alt+Shift Glances), or Open Link in Peek from its right-click menu.

- **Alt+Q** looks up the text you've selected. With nothing selected, it opens a small prompt.
- **Shift+Enter** in the URL bar peeks what you typed instead of going there.
- **Escape** closes a Peek. **Ctrl+Enter** keeps it as a normal tab.
- Peeks are a floating page, not a tab, and they stay out of your history.
- **Prefixes**: `m` maps, `w` Wikipedia, `d` define, `t` translate, `wt` weather, `y` YouTube, `ai` Google AI Mode, `$` currency, `gh` GitHub, `r` Reddit. Type `?` in Peek to see them all. Edit, add or remove prefixes right there.
- **Instant answers**: maths, units and time zones show right in the prompt, worked out offline by Firefox itself. Enter copies the answer.
- **Answers from the web**: `$ 100 usd eur` (or just `100 usd to eur`) converts currency, `d word` shows a definition, `wt city` the weather (`wt` alone: the weather near you, with the places you looked up lately underneath), all in the prompt. Weather units follow your country, or the "Peek weather units" setting. Enter copies a conversion or opens the full page. Switch these off in settings if you'd rather nothing is looked up as you type.
- **Timers**: `timer 10m tea` starts a countdown, `pomo` a pomodoro (`pomo 15 10 4`, or `pomo focus 10, 5 break, 3 rounds`, for your own pattern), and `timer` lists them (Delete cancels). A running timer drains down the left edge of the page, and a small card in the corner says when it ends.

**Switcher**: **Alt+W** opens a list over the page. Type a few letters of a tab's title or site (in any space), a folder or a space (in any order, with gaps: `gh zen`), and Enter goes there, switching space if it has to. With nothing typed, the tabs you used last come first. Start with `:` for commands: `:focus`, `:compact`, `:settings`, `:tabs`, `:set frost`, `:timer 10m tea`, `:pomo`, `:peek something`, `:colours` (`:colours wallpaper`, `:colours undo`), `:routing`.

**Tab manager**: **Alt+Shift+T** (or `:tabs`) opens `about:zenith-tabs`, in four views. **All tabs**: cards for where the memory and CPU go (the total and CPU now and over the last minute, with Sleep other spaces; the heaviest sites; each space, with Sleep and Wake; tabs idle for an hour or more; tabs working hard; duplicates), then every tab in a table: its space, where it is (pinned, a folder, essential), awake or asleep, memory, CPU now and its average over the last minute, and when you last used it, sorted by any of them (click a heading; beside the tick box, picked tabs first) and filtered (awake, asleep, this space, idle, playing, picked). Tabs of one site share a process, so their memory is shared out between them; a sleeping tab uses next to nothing. **Spaces**: each space is a column of its tabs as its sidebar has them: folders (a pin) and folders that don't pin, folders in folders, then loose tabs, with its essentials on top, and Sleep and Wake for the whole space. Each awake tab shows its memory (and CPU when busy); the header adds them up. Tick tabs (a click ticks, Shift ticks a range, Ctrl+A all, double-click goes to one) or a folder's box for all its tabs, and the bar at the bottom moves them to a space (a pinned tab stays pinned, a tab leaves its folder, a split view moves whole), puts them in a new folder (pinned or not) or an existing one, takes them out of their folder, puts them to sleep (Zen's own pages too, Settings and this one), wakes them or closes them. Every change can be undone (the note that comes up, the Undo button, Ctrl+Z; undoing a new folder takes the folder away), a folder left empty is offered for deleting, and reopening closed tabs keeps you on the page. Drag tabs onto a space or a folder, or a folder onto another space. Undo (the button) lists your recent changes: pick one to go back to before it; it says what it put back, or that something was already as it was. **Tidy**: duplicates, tabs you haven't used for 3, 7, 14 or 30 days, awake tabs heaviest first, busy sites, and by topic (with the local AI): each lists its tabs ticked; untick what to keep, then press its button. A card that puts tabs into a folder says where (a new folder, its name yours to change, or your folder of that name, which they join), and what was done shows under Just done (Show, Undo). **Rules** puts tabs into folders as they arrive (a site, an address or a title, in any space or one, into a folder you link it to, or one with the rule's name, made again if you deleted it), and can put tabs to sleep or close loose ones after a while; nothing acts on its own until you set it. 

**Bookmarks**: **Ctrl+B** (or `:bookmarks`) opens them in Zenith's look instead of Firefox's sidebar: floating over the right of the page (default), docked on the right so the page makes room, or on the left beside the tabs; drag its inner edge to resize. Rows like the tab list (folders open in place) or tiles (folders you step into), with search. Your bookmarks toolbar comes first, then Bookmarks Menu and Other Bookmarks. "This space first" shows a section for the space you're in: its own folder (made when you first drop something there) and the bookmarks Zen's edit dialog gives to that space; ones given only to other spaces are left out, as in Zen's menus. Drop a tab or link on it to bookmark it (on a folder to put it there), drag bookmarks onto folders to move them, right-click to edit, keep with this space or delete. The same view is a section of Zen's Library, and Firefox's Library window (Manage bookmarks) takes Zenith's colours. "Firefox's tree, restyled" keeps Firefox's own sidebar instead, in Zenith's colours, on the side you chose.

**Link hints**: **Alt+J** puts a few letters on every link, button and field you can see in the page. Type a label to follow it (a field gets the cursor), Backspace takes a letter back, Esc (or any other key, a click or scrolling) stops. **Alt+Shift+J** opens the link in a new tab in the background instead. Keys › Link hints.

**Float a tab**: **Alt+Shift+F** (or right-click the tab › Float this tab, beside Duplicate) moves the tab you're on (Zen's own pages and essentials too; an essential comes back an essential) into a small window of its own (bottom right of the screen; no title bar or window buttons, and as small as you like); the page carries on as it was, nothing reloads. The window takes the focus, so Alt+Shift+F again puts the tab back. Keys › Float a tab › Stays above: **its Zen window only** (the default: not above other apps; it closes with that window: Ctrl+Shift+T in another window floats it again, and if Zen was quitting, it floats again as Zen starts) or **every app**. Its bar: the page's title (click it, or Ctrl+L, Alt+D or F4, to type an address or a search), a pin (pinned, the bar sits above the page; unpinned, it slides down when the pointer comes into the window or to its top edge), back into the window, minimise (above every app), and close (Ctrl+Shift+T brings it back, floating). The pin button shows what it does (a pin struck through unpins). Drag the bar to move it (it settles against an edge you let go near; double-click for its corner). A floating tab floats again after Zen restarts, however Zen was closed.

**Start page**: `about:zenith-start`, on the window's glass: a clock and the date, a search box (Peek's engine) that also finds your bookmarks as you type (arrows and Enter open one, Alt+Enter searches instead, Shift+Enter opens in a new tab), and your bookmarks (toolbar first) on the number keys 1–9 while the box is empty. With the weather on, a line for where you are, then (with its own switch) the places you looked up in Peek lately, each place once. Optionally your most visited sites as tiles (Firefox's own ranking), and the bookmarks can be switched off. It becomes the home page (the Home button, Alt+Home, new windows that open the home page), and with "Home page and new tabs", what Ctrl+T opens instead of Zen's URL bar; Off gives yours back. Pages › Start page.

**Picture-in-picture**: the mouse wheel over it makes it bigger or smaller (the same shape, growing away from the nearest screen edges); let go of it near a screen edge and it settles against it; drop it more than halfway past the left or right edge and it tucks away, a sliver with an arrow staying on screen while it plays. Rest the pointer on the sliver for a moment (or click it) and it comes out, its controls working; it goes back when you click anywhere else, or when the pointer stays away for a few seconds. Panels › Media › Within: the screen, or the Zen window (it stays inside it, moves with it, and is above that window only, not other apps). Its window's corners (Windows 11) and its glass controls follow Look › Corners. Fullscreen is left alone. Drag it away from the edge to keep it out. Nothing moves while the pointer merely passes by. Panels › Media › Move and tuck.

**Palette sync**: with pywal (winwal on Windows) or wallust picking colours from your wallpaper, every space (or only the one you're in: Palette colours) takes the palette's most striking colours (one, two or three) the moment it changes. pywal writes `~/.cache/wal/colors.json` itself; for wallust, add a template that writes the same file to `~/.cache/wallust/colors.json`. `:colours undo` puts every space back. Look › Colour and shape › Palette sync.

**Local AI** (off by default): Firefox's own AI, running on your computer (Zen turns it off; Zenith turns it on while this is on and gives it back after). Each model is fetched from Mozilla once, the first time it's needed. Speed and AI › Local AI.

- **Download names**: when a download with a name that says nothing finishes (`IMG_20241012_0931.jpg`, `8f3a2c1e.pdf`, `download (3).zip`), a small language model (SmolLM2, about 380 MB) names it from the page it came from. Offered on a notice (Rename, Keep), or given at once with Undo. Names that already say something are left alone.
- **By topic** in the tab manager: sorts this space's loose tabs into groups by what they're about, each named for it (Firefox's smart tab groups models, about 60 MB), to put into folders or groups like a busy site. Nothing moves until you press a button.

**Speed**: Zenith's settings are read straight from their preferences (`@media -moz-pref()`), so the browser never searches the window for them; the few things the styles react to deep in the window (the selected tab's container, a tab sharing your camera, the sidebar sliding out) are copied onto the window by Plus as they change. Nothing in Plus measures or polls while its feature is off or out of sight, and the wallpaper is drawn by a worker, away from the window. **Fast start** (Speed and AI › Starting Zen, off by default): the look, the keys and the page you were on come first; the switcher's extras, Peek's housekeeping, the tab manager's rules and the other extras start a part at a time once the page has loaded (within a few seconds). Use one early and it starts at once.

**Cookies** (Zenith › Cookies, `about:zenith-cookies`, `:cookies`): banners answered as they appear, in pages and their frames: Reject (or Only necessary) pressed on the common consent tools (OneTrust, Cookiebot, Didomi, Quantcast, Usercentrics, Sourcepoint, TrustArc and more) and, on any other banner, a button that says no in a few languages; nothing is ever accepted. A banner with no way to refuse is hidden (Reject, or hide when there's no Reject), or left (Reject only); or banners are hidden without an answer; or left alone, everywhere or on sites you name. Which cookies sites may set (Firefox's own setting: its default, no cross-site, no trackers, or only the sites you allow) and how long they're kept (until they expire, or until you close Zen, except sites you allow: only cookies and site data are cleared). The page lists every site with cookies: its rule (as your choices say, Allow, Until you close Zen, Block), its banners (answered or left alone) and Delete for its cookies and data, with the site you came from on top and what was done to banners lately. **Notices with Undo** (on): the first time Zenith refuses or hides a banner on a site, or blocks its cookies beyond what Firefox blocks on its own, a notice says so: Undo shows the banner again (a refusal is forgotten and the page reloads) and leaves the site's banners to you; Allow on this site lets its cookies through. Told once per site and thing while Zen runs; nothing from private windows is kept, not even in the page's Banners lately.

**Focus mode**: **Alt+Z** puts everything away but the page: the sidebar (compact mode, if it wasn't on), the edges, the minimap, pills, the bookmarks bar and the link address hide, and whatever's open closes (Zenith's bookmarks, Firefox's sidebar, Zen's Library, the settings panel, the switcher, the find bar, menus), to open again as you leave. You can bring any of them back while focused. The top toolbar hides too, when Zen shows one. A video site becomes a place to watch (Keys › Focus mode › Video sites): on YouTube the home page is a search page (the logo, the search box with its microphone, your picture; the search dropdown shows Show suggestions instead of suggestions and past searches, typed or not), recommendations, the preview that plays on hover, Shorts (a Short you go to opens as a plain video; on one you're already watching, scrolling to the next is blocked, and its buttons and the next one below are hidden), end screens, autoplay, the channel row, like and share buttons, counts, the player's channel and info buttons, Create and the bell go, the video gets the room (theatre, as tall as the window, everything else under it), and you choose what stays (search, the description and chapters, the playlist you're in, comments folded behind a button, subscriptions); elsewhere, blocks of recommended videos go. The page too (Keys › Focus mode › Pages in focus mode): an article gets a calm reading view drawn over the page (Firefox's own article parser), and any other page that's mostly reading is tidied: cookie and consent banners, newsletter boxes, share bars, comments, related and sponsored blocks, ads, side columns, and bars stuck to the top or bottom of the window go (never anything holding much of the page's text). One pill says focus mode is on, then what was done, with Show the page and Not on <site> (Sites left alone, a list in Zenith's settings). The sharing alarm still shows. Alt+Z again brings everything back, pages as they were, without reloading. It's for the site you're on (Focus mode › On for): on another site's tab the browser is as it was; or for every site. On YouTube, always: the previews that play over a video can't go picture-in-picture.

**Keys**: every Zenith key is in Zen's own Settings › Keyboard shortcuts (under Other, "Zenith: …"), to change or clear like Zen's: Alt+W, Alt+Q, Alt+Z, Alt+Shift+T, Alt+Shift+Z, Alt+J, Alt+Shift+J, Alt+Shift+F (float; 0.12.1's Alt+Shift+Space, which Windows keeps for the window menu, moves over by itself).

**Settings**: Plus adds a **Zenith** page to Zen's Settings: every option with a sentence on what it does, switches, small pictures for looks, and presets that fill in the settings they cover (it says "Custom" once you change one). Press **Alt+Shift+Z** for the same settings as a live panel over the page you're on, or type `set` and a few words in Peek (`set frost`): Tab changes the setting there and then, Enter opens it in the live panel, and the last row searches Zen's own settings. Options that another setting makes irrelevant are greyed out, with the reason; in Sine's mods page your installed mods come first and the marketplace folds behind a button (searching opens it).

**Window**: the window material behind Zen (Windows: Acrylic, Mica, Mica Alt; macOS: its materials; Linux: see-through), with Wallpaper as the default on Windows: your desktop wallpaper drawn behind the window by Zenith, each screen's own, so Frost and Window transparency apply to the docked sidebar too. See-through Zen pages (off by default) lets Settings, Add-ons and Zen's other own pages show that glass too; web pages stay as they are. With Glass edge on Liquid, glass lights up softly under the pointer as you hover, and brighter as you press (tabs, essentials, the media card, Peek, notices).

**What it touches:** the scripts run inside the browser with full access, like any Zen script mod. They collect nothing and send nothing about you. The network requests they cause:

- the pages you choose to Peek;
- web answers in Peek, once you've typed a `$`, `d` or `wt` lookup (or `100 usd to eur`): the currencies go to Frankfurter (European Central Bank rates), the word to Wiktionary, the place to wttr.in (for `wt` alone, wttr.in works out roughly where you are from your connection, as any website can; your recent places are kept on your computer and refreshed when you open `wt`);
- the cover art of what's playing, fetched from the site that's playing it, without cookies.
